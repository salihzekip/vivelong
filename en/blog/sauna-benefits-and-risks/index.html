Sauna Benefits: The Type of Evidence Matters

After our detailed look at Sauna and Longevity, this article covers benefits and risks in one place. An important note: the data on benefits are mostly observational, comparing frequent and infrequent sauna users; that does not prove sauna causes the benefit.

What the Observational Data Show

In the Kuopio study published in JAMA Internal Medicine in 2015, 2,315 Finnish men aged 42-60 were followed for a median of 20.7 years.

  • Sudden cardiac death: adjusted hazard ratio 0.37 (95% CI 0.18-0.75) for 4-7 sessions per week vs 1 session; 0.78 for 2-3 sessions (not significant).
  • Other outcomes: similar associations were reported for fatal coronary heart disease, cardiovascular death and all-cause mortality.
  • Duration: for sessions longer than 19 minutes, the sudden cardiac death hazard ratio was 0.48 vs sessions under 11 minutes; there was no significant duration association for all-cause mortality.
  • Dementia: in a 2017 analysis of the same cohort in Age and Ageing, the dementia hazard ratio for 4-7 sessions per week was 0.34 (95% CI 0.16-0.71).

This cohort consists only of middle-aged Finnish men in a society with a sauna culture. Frequent sauna use may also be a marker of other healthy habits, which the study cannot fully separate.

Risks and Precautions

According to a review in the American Journal of Medicine (Hannuksela and Ellahham, 2001), sauna is well tolerated by most healthy adults, but:

  • Contraindications: unstable angina pectoris, recent myocardial infarction and severe aortic stenosis are listed as contraindications. For many people with stable angina or an old heart attack, it was found safe.
  • Alcohol: alcohol during sauna raises the risk of hypotension, arrhythmia and sudden death and should be avoided.
  • Skin: sweating can increase itching in atopic dermatitis.
  • Fertility (men): the review states sauna does not influence fertility; however, in a 2013 Human Reproduction study of 10 men, two 15-minute sessions a week at 80-90°C for three months markedly reduced sperm count and motility, and the effect was reversible. The difference between these two findings cannot be settled here; men planning a child should consult a physician.

In pregnancy, cardiovascular disease, low blood pressure or regular medication use, make the sauna decision together with a physician.

Limitations

  • The main benefit data are observational and come from men in a single country.
  • Sauna frequency and duration were self-reported.
  • The figures for session length and frequency are observed associations, not recommendations.
  • The sperm study is very small (10 people); results cannot be generalized.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is sauna good for you?

In observational studies, frequent sauna use was associated with lower sudden cardiac death and dementia risk (in Finnish men, 4-7 sessions per week vs 1). This is not proof of causation.

Can sauna be harmful?

It is well tolerated by most healthy adults, but is contraindicated in unstable angina, recent heart attack and severe aortic stenosis; alcohol during sauna raises the risk of hypotension and arrhythmia.

Does sauna affect male fertility?

One review says it does not, but a 10-man study found that two sessions per week for three months reversibly reduced sperm count and motility. Men planning a child should consult a physician.

How often and how long should you use a sauna?

Observational studies linked more frequent (4-7 per week) and longer (over 19 minutes) sessions with lower risk, but that is an association, not advice. Ask a physician for personal guidance.

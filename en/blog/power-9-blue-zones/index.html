What Is Power 9?

Journalist and researcher Dan Buettner, with support from National Geographic, spent years studying the world's five longest-living communities — Sardinia, Okinawa, Nicoya, Ikaria, and Loma Linda. These communities live on different continents, practice different religions, and eat different cuisines — they share no common genetics. Yet their ways of life show a striking overlap: Buettner distilled these commonalities into nine points and called them "Power 9."

The most important thing about Power 9 is this: none of it says "go to the gym" or "follow a special diet." Every principle is ordinary enough to be a natural part of daily life — which is exactly what makes it sustainable.

The Nine Principles

  • 1. Natural Movement — Sardinia & Nicoya: Shepherds in Sardinia's Barbagia Valley herd livestock across an average of 8 km of mountainous terrain a day; Nicoyan farmers keep up "sabanza," hand-worked farming. None of them call it "exercise" — movement is a natural part of the day's work. See the Sardinia and Nicoya pages for detail.
  • 2. Purpose — Okinawa & Nicoya: Okinawans call it "ikigai," Nicoyans call it "plan de vida" — the answer to "why do I get up in the morning?" This awareness has been linked to up to 7 additional years of life expectancy. See the Okinawa page.
  • 3. Downshift — 4 Regions, 4 Different Rituals: Okinawans spend a few minutes each day honoring their ancestors, Loma Linda's Adventists pray, Ikarians take a midday siesta, Sardinians hold their evening "happy hour" in the village square — four different forms of the same daily "reset" ritual.
  • 4. The 80% Rule (Hara Hachi Bu) — Okinawa: A 2,500-year-old Confucian mantra recited aloud before meals: stop eating when your stomach is 80% full. See the Okinawa page for detail.
  • 5. Plant-Slant Diet — Nicoya's "Three Sisters": What Nicoyans call "las tres hermanas" (the three sisters) — corn, beans and squash — makes up most of what's on their plate. Beans are a shared protein source across all 5 regions. Detailed principles: Longevity Nutrition guide.
  • 6. Moderate Alcohol — Sardinia's Cannonau Wine: 4 of the 5 regions drink 1-2 glasses a day, with food and friends — Sardinia's antioxidant-rich Cannonau wine especially. Loma Linda's Adventists are the exception; spirituality fills that role instead. See the Sardinia page.
  • 7. Belong — Loma Linda: Loma Linda's Seventh-day Adventists attend religious services weekly; nearly all of the 263 centenarians studied belonged to a faith community. Regular attendance at services has been linked to 4–14 additional years of life expectancy. See the Loma Linda page.
  • 8. Loved Ones First — Sardinia & Okinawa: In Sardinia and Okinawa, aging parents move in with their children — or right next door — rather than into a separate care facility. Successful centenarians keep their grandchildren close by, a safety net that's both emotional and practical.
  • 9. Right Tribe — Okinawa's "Moai" System: From childhood, Okinawans join lifelong friendship groups of five called "moai," offering each other financial and emotional support for life. Your social circle shapes your health behaviors. See the Social Connection & Purpose guide.

To Be Honest: Blue Zones Data Is Controversial

In 2024, UCL demographer Saul Newman, in a study that won an Ig Nobel Prize, argued that some Blue Zone supercentenarian (100+ and especially 110+) statistics may stem from missing birth certificates, record-keeping errors and even pension fraud. His claim: the highest rates of "extreme old age" correlated with low birth-certificate registration rates and high poverty.

Power 9 creator Dan Buettner and his team pushed back hard; in a 2025 paper published in The Gerontologist, they said the data had been independently verified through church records, and birth/marriage/military/death registers. One interesting detail: that verification paper doesn't cover Loma Linda, the most disputed of the five regions — Buettner later acknowledged Loma Linda was added to the list because "the National Geographic editor wanted a region from the Americas."

As Vivelong, we want to state this transparently: the debate is centered mainly on the statistic of "how many people really lived past 100" — it doesn't debunk the health benefits of the individual Power 9 behaviors (plant-slant eating, regular movement, social connection, sense of purpose), which are supported separately by a much broader body of epidemiological literature (e.g. the Harvard Study of Adult Development), independent of the Blue Zones controversy. But the claim that "an incredible number of people over 100 live in these 5 villages" shouldn't be repeated as uncritically as it once was.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Blue Zones data really controversial?

Yes. In 2024, demographer Saul Newman won an Ig Nobel Prize for arguing that some supercentenarian statistics may stem from record-keeping errors and missing birth certificates. Dan Buettner and his team formally rebutted this in a 2025 paper in The Gerontologist, citing official records — but that paper doesn't cover Loma Linda, the most disputed region. The debate is confined to "how many people really lived past 100"; the health benefits of the Power 9 behavioral recommendations are supported by separate literature.

Does following the Power 9 principles actually extend life?

Power 9 emerged not from a single study but as the common thread across dozens of demographic and epidemiological studies in the five Blue Zones — so we're talking about strong correlation, not proven causation. A sense of purpose has been linked to up to 7 additional years, and regular attendance at services to 4–14 additional years, of life expectancy. These are observational findings — no "exact X years longer" guarantee — but a strong signal where millennia of community experience meet modern epidemiology.

Which Power 9 principle is the most effective?

There isn't a single "most effective" principle — Power 9's strength comes from all nine working together, woven into daily life. The two that stand out most: Purpose (linked to up to 7 additional years of life expectancy) and Social Connection (lets healthy behaviors spread "contagiously" through your circle). Vivelong's advice: start with the 1-2 principles that feel easiest, rather than trying to change everything at once.

Loma Linda Adventists don't drink alcohol — so how do they fit Power 9?

The "Moderate Alcohol" principle applies in 4 of the 5 regions — Loma Linda's Seventh-day Adventists are the exception, since their faith prohibits alcohol. This shows Power 9 isn't one rigid formula: the nine principles manifest differently region to region. In Loma Linda, a stronger sense of spirituality and community fills that role instead — same outcome (a regular, community-based downshifting ritual), different vehicle.
